## Other Insights

- **Kevin started with the person, then moved to the incident.** "How are you doing?" came first, and "You look tired" led to Friday afternoon off. Both show attention to Sam, not only to the incident.
  - *Leadership lens (Carnegie, Part 2 #1: "Become genuinely interested in other people"):* the wellbeing check wasn't a warm-up. It came back later in the meeting with a concrete action.

- **The praise was specific enough to use as evidence.** "An update every fifteen minutes… three customers missed their pay-run cutoff, and not one of them escalated to Dana." Sam's reply ("I didn't think anyone noticed that part") shows how rarely this kind of work gets seen.
  - *Leadership lens (Carnegie, Part 1 #2: "Give honest and sincere appreciation"):* it came from actually listening to the bridge recording, which is what made it sincere rather than generic.

- **Kevin moved blame off Sam three times.** "That's not a you problem." "That's a process gap, not a Sam gap." "You're not complaining. You found it." Sam was worried about looking like he was criticizing a colleague's action item, and Kevin turned that into credit.
  - *Leadership lens (Carnegie, Part 4 #5: "Let the other person save face"):* Sam can keep raising uncomfortable findings without feeling like the complainer.

- **Kevin confirmed that Sam's feeling bad was reasonable and turned it into a compliment.** "It should feel bad. That's part of what makes you good at this."
  - *Leadership lens (Carnegie, Part 4 #7: "Give the other person a fine reputation to live up to"):* it treats Sam's sense of ownership as a strength to keep.

- **The growth plan came from Sam.** Kevin asked "How do you want to grow out of this one?", Sam named the gap ("reading runbooks like a tourist"), and only then did Kevin suggest pairing.
  - *Leadership lens (Carnegie, Part 4 #4: "Ask questions instead of giving direct orders"; Part 3 #7: "Let the other person feel that the idea is his or hers"):* Sam is more likely to follow through on a plan he helped shape.

- **Coaching opportunity: don't absorb the whole fix.** Taking the duplicate action items to Dana is the right escalation. But if Kevin also takes on the fix, Sam loses the clearest route to his 40% goal ("own one preventive fix end to end"). When you raise it with Dana, **credit Sam by name as the one who found it**, which also answers Dana's ask to make the team's wins more visible. Then offer Sam the ProcWatch paging fix as a stretch.
  - *Leadership lens (Carnegie, Part 3 #12: "Throw down a challenge"; Part 2 #6: "Make the other person feel important—and do it sincerely"):* public credit and a real challenge build more than a handled escalation does.

- **The closing thanks rewarded work nobody had asked for.** "Nobody asked you to, and it's the most useful thing anyone did after that incident." Re-reading old RCAs is the behavior that stops repeat incidents, so it's worth reinforcing out loud.
  - *Leadership lens (Carnegie, Part 4 #6: "Praise the slightest improvement and praise every improvement. Be 'hearty in your approbation and lavish in your praise'"):*

- **Pattern worth watching:** postmortem action items can be duplicated and dropped without anyone noticing. ACT-110 was overdue for about a year and ACT-160 copied it. If one fix slipped through, others may have too. A quick audit of open RCA action items for duplicates and overdue items could be worth proposing to Dana, and it's the same kind of matching the AI pilot is meant to do.

- **Manager-only note:** Sam mentioned poor sleep the week of the incident. Check in lightly at the next 1:1 and watch Sam's on-call load. Leave this out of any version shared with Sam or others.
